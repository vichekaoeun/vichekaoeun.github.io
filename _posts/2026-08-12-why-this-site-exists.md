I wanted a place that felt closer to a notebook than a portfolio.

Portfolios are polished by design. They compress months of wrong turns into one tidy paragraph and a link to the repository. That has its place—but most of the useful parts disappear in the edit: the assumptions that failed, the strange bug that took an afternoon, and the one sentence in the documentation that finally made everything click.

This site is where those parts go.

## What I’ll write about

Expect notes on systems software, reliability, developer tools, security, and whatever I happen to be building. Some entries will be complete essays. Others will be short field notes written while the details are still fresh.

There will also be books, films, games, and occasional observations that have nothing to do with computers. A personal site should be allowed to contain a person.

## A useful constraint

I’m keeping the machinery intentionally simple. Every post is a Markdown file committed beside the rest of the site. There is no dashboard, database, or publishing platform between the writing and the page you’re reading.

That simplicity is a constraint, but a good one. It makes publishing feel like saving a note—and means the words will remain portable long after this design changes.
