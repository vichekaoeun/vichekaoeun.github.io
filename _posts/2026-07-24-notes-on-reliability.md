---
title: "Reliability is a user experience"
description: "The best reliability work is often visible only as an absence of surprise."
category: Reliability
---

We usually describe reliability with numbers: availability, latency percentiles, recovery time. These are useful because they turn a vague quality into something a team can observe.

The user, however, experiences none of those numbers directly. They experience confidence—or the lack of it.

A reliable system behaves consistently enough that people can stop thinking about it. It communicates clearly when something goes wrong, preserves their work, and returns to a known state. In that sense, reliability is part of the interface. Error messages, fallbacks, retries, and status pages are design materials just as surely as type and color are.

This is why reliability work cannot end at keeping a process alive. A service returning fast, technically valid nonsense is available but not useful. The meaningful question is whether the system keeps the promises its users believe it made.
